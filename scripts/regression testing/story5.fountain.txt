Title: THE STORIES THAT DON'T STAY IN THE GLASS
Author: AI Assistant
Format: Fountain

EXT. MUSEUM - DAY

A grand, imposing stone building. Towering marble pillars. 

Heavy wooden doors creak open. A mother and daughter enter.

INT. MUSEUM - FOYER - CONTINUOUS

MIA (7) steps inside. She is small, wide-eyed, wearing scuffed sneakers and a bright yellow cardigan. 

Her mother, ELLEN (30s), walks briskly beside her, clutching a canvas tote bag.

The atmosphere is heavy, sacred, and dead silent. The faint echo of footsteps sounds like the rustle of turning pages.

ELLEN
(without looking down)
Don’t touch anything, Mia.

Mia looks around. 

EXHIBIT CASES line the walls. Inside: polished, iridescent minerals; ancient silver coins; a faded, clockwork tin monkey. 

Mia’s hands twitch. She reaches out a finger, stopping a millimeter away from the glass. 

MIA (V.O.)
It felt strange. Everything looked like it wanted to be held. I wondered if they were lonely behind the glass.

INT. MUSEUM - DINOSAUR HALL - MOMENTS LATER

Mia stands frozen. 

An immense CHROMEOSAURUS SKELETON towers over her. The rib cage arches like cathedral beams, stretching to the vaulted ceiling.

Other VISITORS pass by, eyes glued to small information plaques, whispering quietly.

Mia ignores the plaques. She looks up into the empty eye sockets of the skull. 

MIA (V.O.)
Did they have a favorite plant to munch on? A friendasaurus to walk beside? Did they dream at night?

Mia steps over the velvet rope. She pushes up on her tiptoes. She reaches her small hand toward the massive toe bone.

The shadows shift. The massive skull tilts down, a fraction of an inch, as if leaning in to whisper a secret.

Mia gasps. She holds her breath.

ELLEN (O.S.)
Mia! Come along.

Mia blinks. The skull is perfectly still again—just dry, ancient plaster and wire. 

Mia steps back over the rope and runs to catch up.

INT. MUSEUM - ARTIFACT ROOM - CONTINUOUS

Mia stands before a low glass pedestal. 

Inside is an unglazed, clay SOUP BOWL. A dark, jagged crack runs directly down its center.

Mia stares at it. Her vision blurs.

INT. MUSEUM - ARTIFACT ROOM - PRESENT

Mia smiles at the cracked bowl.

MIA (V.O.)
Did they know that this one mistake would be remembered?

INT. MUSEUM - HISTORY WING - LATER

Mia stops in front of a small, isolated display case. 

Inside rests a single, weathered CHILD'S LEATHER SHOE. It is worn at the heel, exactly Mia's size.

Mia looks down at her own sneaker. She lifts her foot, mimicking a skip. 

MIA (V.O.)
The child who wore it had run somewhere once. Skipped, stumbled, kicked stones. Did they jump in puddles like me?

Mia’s smile fades. A heavy realization hits her. She presses her palm flat against the cold glass of the case.

MIA (V.O.)
It made me feel funny... knowing someone my size could become something people just look at through glass.

INT. MUSEUM - PORTRAIT GALLERY - LATER

A long hallway lined with massive, gold-framed oil paintings. The subjects look stiff, stern, and deeply aristocratic.

Mia walks down the center line, inspecting them. 

She stops at a portrait of a STERN MAN with silver buttons. Mia squints. 

In the painting, the man’s pocket bulges slightly. A tiny, painted LADYBUG crawls near his cuff. Mia winks at him.

She moves to a portrait of a WOMAN IN A BLUE DRESS. The woman’s painted expression is rigid, but her feet are positioned as if mid-waltz.

Mia beams a massive, warm smile directly at the canvas. 

MIA (V.O.)
I smiled at each serious face, just in case smiling could travel through time. Maybe they will smile back next time.

EXT. STREET - LATER (DUSK)

The sun is setting, casting a warm golden glow over the pavement. 

Ellen walks down the sidewalk, holding Mia’s hand. Mia skips alongside her, kicking a stray pebble.

ELLEN
So, which part of the museum was your favorite?

MIA
The stories that don’t stay in the glass.

Ellen stops walking. She looks down at her daughter, genuinely puzzled.

ELLEN
What do you mean by that?

MIA
The people leave, but they take the stories home with them. Everything has a story, Mum. Even shoes and bowls.

Mia pulls her mother's hand, leading her forward. 

Ellen watches her daughter skip ahead, a new softness in her eyes. She looks down at her own canvas bag, then back at Mia, smiling.
