What ScriptLens tracks

Every scene introduces things the story carries forward: props, objects, characters, setups. ScriptLens builds a dependency graph — "Scene 3 only works because Scene 1 introduced the revolver."

That graph powers all three features below.

1. Orphan scenes

"These are scenes the rest of the script never looks back at."

What it means

A scene is an orphan when no later scene depends on it. Nothing downstream references what that scene introduced. It may be cuttable — or it may need a stronger tie-in.

Example

INT. A - DAY
MARCUS holds a LEDGER.

INT. B - DAY
A lone STATUE sits in the dark.

INT. C - DAY
MARCUS reads the ledger.
SceneWhat happensOrphan?

Scene 3Marcus reads the ledgerNo — depends on Scene 1

  • Scene 2 flagged in the scene list
  • Click orphans → jump to Scene 2
"You've got a beautiful statue scene, but the rest of the script never pays it off. ScriptLens flags that immediately — before your producer asks why it's in the cut."

Writer actions

  • Cut it (safe candidate)
  • Add a callback later
  • Merge it into a scene that already carries story weight

2. Simulate cut

"Cut with confidence. See the blast radius before you touch Final Draft."

What it means

Pick a scene, click Simulate cut. ScriptLens shows every downstream scene that would lose its setup. Your file is never modified — it's a preview only.

Example: cut Scene 2

Scene 3Marcus aims the revolver, but the "grab" in Scene 2 is goneScene 2 → Scene 3

Cut Scene 1 instead → both Scene 2 and Scene 3 break.

What the client sees

  • Center: Scene 2 as ghost/strikethrough — "Simulated removal"
  • Right panel: impact list with dependency paths
  • Go to scene links — one click to broken payoffs
  • Banner: "Simulation only — your script is unchanged."
"Writers cut constantly in development. Usually you find out three scenes later that something doesn't land. ScriptLens shows you that in one click."
Producer analogy: "It's like deleting a row in a spreadsheet and seeing which formulas break — but for story logic."

3. Simulate edit

"You don't have to cut a whole scene to break the story — sometimes you just rewrite one line."

What it means

Edit a scene's text, then click Simulate edit. ScriptLens re-parses with your change and shows edges removed, scenes at risk, and orphan count before vs. after.

Example: remove the revolver from Scene 1

Original:

INT. ROOM ONE - DAY

FAQ

Does ScriptLens change my script?

No. All features are read-only previews.

Simulate cut vs. simulate edit?

Cut = remove entire scene. Edit = rewrite one scene's content.

What makes a scene an orphan?

No incoming dependency edges from later scenes (excluding the opening scene).

Supported formats?

Fountain, PDF, FDX.

Open in browser → File → Print → Save as PDF for a shareable deck handout.
